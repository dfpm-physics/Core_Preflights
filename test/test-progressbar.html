Beyond the bar — show the spread, not just the mean

An average of 2.5 can be “everyone’s mediocre” or “half aced it, half are lost.” These give faculty the shape of the class at a glance — whether to reteach, extend, or chase a few outliers.

Spread · your colors
Histogram pill ⭐
Your five favorite zones — but each zone’s height is how many students landed there. Same bar, now it shows the spread. The line marks the class mean.
Why: this is your liked bar and a histogram at once — a tall green end means most of the class is there; a tall red end means reteach. No new color vocabulary to learn.
Spread · your colors
Histogram — fine cells (5×5)
Same histogram, but each color region is split into five thin same-color cells (separated-cells style) for a finer, more textured silhouette.
Why: the extra subdivisions read as a smoother distribution curve while keeping your five colors and the at-a-glance “where’s the class massed” story.
Spread · your colors
Composition pill
Same five colors as one pill; each color’s width = the share of the class at that level.
Why: the most compact spread view — fits a single row, yet still answers “how much of my class actually has this?”
Wild
Distribution histogram (per score)
Mini column chart of how many students scored 0…5 — six discrete steps.
Why: the finest-grained look; exposes bimodality (two humps = a split class) that any average hides.
Wild
Student dot strip
One dot per student along the 0–5 axis; the line marks the class mean.
Why: faculty see actual people and outliers — “these four are stranded at 0/1” — great for deciding who to follow up with.
Tame
Bullet chart (mean vs. target)
Faint mastery bands behind a measure bar, with a target tick (3.5/5).
Why: the classic dashboard metric — is this objective above or below where I want it? The target line makes “good enough” explicit.
Wild
Class profile radar
Every objective on one spider; solid = class mean, dashed = target. The whole lesson in one shape.
Why: shape-recognition — a dented corner is the weak objective; compare lessons by silhouette at a glance.
Tame-ish
Radial rings
A filled ring per objective — your “separate boxes,” gone circular.
Why: punchy and scannable for a headline strip; the number lives in the middle and the color says “good/bad” before you read it.
